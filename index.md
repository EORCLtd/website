## Most decisions have to be made before the future is revealed.

Understanding uncertainty: defining uncertainty.

Uncertainty is a state of having unknown information about the future. It prevents decision-maker from knowing the consequences of a decision but also comes with great value if managed properly.

**EORC uses scenarios to represent the unknown information to identify the hidden value in uncertainty.**

A scenario tree: from today, the future branches into four scenarios, while a single forecast follows one straight line.

### Significant value is hidden in uncertainty.

Managing uncertainty via scenario modelling help to make the optimal decision considering the expectation of the unknown future. This helps the decision to be more robust than decision made considering only one future. This also helps to find and quantify the value of flexibility.

## Managing uncertainty helps us find the value of flexibility.

What planning with uncertainty looks like. Here is a simple energy storage example showing planning under uncertainty can yield much higher revenue.

### Problem setup

Tomorrow's energy prices are unknown but we know the distribution of them, 110£/unit or 10£/unit with equal probability.

- Buy energy today: £50 per unit
- Store it: £20 per unit
- Battery size: 100 units

Decisions to make:

1. Whether to charge the battery today at £50.
2. Whether to discharge tomorrow at the unknown price, or wait and discharge the day after at a guaranteed £50.

### A. Plan with a deterministic view

Plan with the expected price. The average guess squeezes both outcomes into one number.

The average price is £60, only £10 more than the £50 you paid. Storing costs £20, so it looks like a loss.

#### Don't charge the battery today at all.

### B. Plan for both outcomes

Planning for both outcomes means having an answer for each: at £110 discharge tomorrow, at £10 keep the energy and discharge for £50.

If the price is high, sell tomorrow and earn £60 a unit. If it is low, keep it and earn £0. On average that is £30 a unit, more than the £20 storage cost.

#### Buy 100 units

### What happens

| Case | Result | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High price (50%) | +£4,000 | sell tomorrow at £110 |
| Low price (50%) | -£2,000 | keep it, sell at £50 |
| On average | +£1,000 | not buying earns £0 |

Each case already includes £5,000 to buy the energy and £2,000 to store it.

**£1,000 more on average by planning for both outcomes.** The average misses the value of adapting. Planning for many possible futures like this is called stochastic optimization.

_Illustrative prices in pounds. Storage starts empty, with no energy losses or extra charges. £1,000 is an average; the low-price case loses £2,000. For simplicity, we omit the fee for the fixed-price sale._
