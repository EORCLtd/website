Why uncertainty matters

Nobody knows tomorrow's prices, weather or demand.

Every energy decision is made today, but how it turns out depends on things no one can know yet. A plan built on one forecast only works if that forecast comes true. Planning with scenarios keeps many possible futures in view, so the decision still holds when things turn out differently.

Should we invest in energy storage?

An average-price forecast can hide a profitable opportunity. This simple example shows how modelling possible futures changes the investment decision.

Stored energy can be sold later at a known £50 per unit. We choose capacity and buy energy today, then decide whether to sell tomorrow or hold for that later sale.

Tomorrow's price: £110 or £10 50% chance of each outcome
01Use the average price
Deterministic model
  • Average price: (£110 + £10) / 2 = £60.
  • Trading margin: £60 - £50 = £10.
  • £10 per unit is below the £20 storage cost.

Do not invest

100 units would lose £1,000 in this forecast.
02Model both outcomes
Stochastic model
  • High price: sell at £110; margin £60.
  • Low price: hold for £50; margin £0.
  • Expected margin: (£60 + £0) / 2 = £30.
  • £30 exceeds the £20 storage cost.

Invest in 100 units

Expected net profit: £10 per unit.
